LLM Council Report: Paper Feed Design

Five advisors · anonymous peer review · chairman synthesis

Question brought to the council

Is the paper_feed_current_design.md design sound, internally consistent, and achievable within a semester IRE project (OpenAlex-only, no training, API budget 0, RTX 3050)? What are the biggest flaws, gaps and undervalued pieces, and what should be changed, cut or prioritized before implementation continues?

Chairman's Verdict

The design isn't sound as written, but it can be saved in a semester if you narrow the claim.

Where the council agrees

  • The main evaluation doesn't test the system you designed. LFO author timelines have no sessions, no reading order, no skips and no likes. So V-SKNN decay, the pivot gate γ and the session timeout never actually run.
  • The citation signal mostly tests itself. The +2.0 "user cited p" tier only points at papers already removed from the ground truth (§16.4, §19). The graph also shares its source with the labels.
  • Future data leaks into the past. cited_by_count and co-citation are today's snapshots. Rebuild them from papers up to T and use counts_by_year.
  • Corpus coverage is unchecked. Build the corpus outward from the eval authors' citation neighbourhoods, then report the share of Ref(B) it covers.
  • The doc contradicts itself:
    • The session update differs between §12 and §15.
    • λ is ≈0.5 in one place and [0.7, 0.95] in another.
    • γ and w* each have more than one meaning.
    • The §15 decay gives the latest item a weight of eδ > 1.
    • Novelty is added after MMR, which undoes the diversity step.
    • The 16 GB LLM baseline won't fit on a 4 GB GPU.
    • Feed mode has no text query for BM25 or the reranker.
  • Cut scope. Shrink the user study from n=50 to about 10–15, drop the reranker from the feed path, drop the LLM baseline, and tune about 3 weights instead of about 15.

Where the council clashes

The Expansionist wants to release LFO as a benchmark, add ORCID onboarding, and add facet query operators. All five reviewers called this the biggest blind spot, because publishing a benchmark that leaks and has low coverage makes its flaws public. Two of the Expansionist's ideas hold up:

  • Report re-citations and discoveries separately.
  • Use real topic pivots in Paper B to label the γ gate.

Both should wait until the evaluation harness is fixed.

Blind spots caught in peer review

  • SPECTER2 likely leaks too. It was trained on S2 citation data that probably includes 2023–24, so BM25 is the only clean baseline.
  • Whose history is Paper B? OpenAlex author disambiguation is noisy and multi-author papers have no single "user". Use one seed author per paper (first or single authors).
  • Self-citations in Ref(B) are trivially predictable. Filter them out or report them separately.
  • No statistics. There's no LFO sample size, no confidence intervals and no paired significance tests.
  • Missing baselines: popular recent papers before T, co-author papers, and "re-recommend own references".
  • Other gaps: no ethics or consent plan for the user study, no compute budget for GLiNER and NLI on 4 GB, and no fallback deliverable.

The recommendation

  1. Restate the hypothesis so LFO can test it: faceted persona + time-bounded citation-graph signals beat SPECTER2/BM25 at predicting next-paper references, especially discoveries.
  2. Demote session gating, or test it only on clearly labelled simulated sessions.
  3. Rename V-SKNN, or implement real neighbour-session kNN.
  4. Give the baselines the same candidate pool and MMR so the ablation isn't confounded.
  5. Check that PaperFlow (arXiv:2606.07454) exists and can be reimplemented.

Build order:

  • Weeks 1–3: corpus, LFO harness and BM25/SPECTER2 baselines.
  • Week 4: GLiNER with SciERC evaluation.
  • Then the ranker with 3 signals.
  • Last: ablations with confidence intervals.

NLI verification and the user study are optional extras. Add one notation table and one canonical scoring equation, and fix the draft text and the unclosed code blocks.

The one thing to do first

Write a one-page LFO evaluation spec and settle it before any model code. It should fix:

  • the seed-author rule and the self-citation filter;
  • the cutoff T and the time-bounded features;
  • how the corpus is built and the coverage target;
  • the split into re-citations and discoveries;
  • the number of authors and the significance test.

Everything else depends on what this harness can measure.

Advisor Responses

The ContrarianFatal flaw

The main evaluation (§16.4/§28) doesn't measure the system you designed.

  1. LFO has no sessions. There is no order within a year, so position decay, the pivot gate and the timeout are arbitrary. The +33% simulation claim is circular because a "read" is defined as a cluster match.
  2. The ablation is confounded. Only the proposed model gets citation, facet and MMR signals. "V-SKNN" here is a decayed centroid, not Jannach's session-kNN.
  3. The citation tiers conflict with the ground truth. The +2.0 tier only scores excluded items. If you drop the exclusion, re-recommending old references becomes a trivial baseline that wins.
  4. Leakage. cited_by_count and co-citation are present-day snapshots, so they need year-bounded reconstruction.
  5. Corpus coverage. Much of Ref(B) will fall outside the 100k subset.

Contradictions:

  • §12 and §15 reset differently.
  • λ≈0.5 conflicts with [0.7, 0.95].
  • γ has three meanings.
  • The decay gives a weight of eδ > 1.
  • Novelty after MMR undoes the diversity step.
  • Searching over ~15 weights contradicts §35.
  • A 16 GB LLM won't fit on a 4 GB GPU.
  • The reranker has no query.
  • Exact-match facets will rarely fire.

Cut: the n=50 study (to about 10), NLI verification, the LLM baseline and the reranker.

The First Principles ThinkerWrong claim

What is the main claim, and can your evaluation measure it? Right now it can't.

  1. The ground truth can't measure the main idea. V-PaperFlow models reading sessions, but LFO has no reading events, order or skips. st, Δt and γ are never exercised.
  2. The evaluation is really citation prediction, which §35 puts out of scope. The citation signal also shares a graph with the labels.
  3. The feed has no query. In LFO mode BM25 and RRF have nothing to search with.
  4. Coverage. Build the corpus outward from the eval authors and report the share of ground truth it reaches.

Fix: state one hypothesis LFO can test, e.g. "faceted persona + citation-graph signals beat pure SPECTER2 for next-paper reference recommendation". Cut session gating, or test it on labelled simulated sessions. Settle §15 vs §17.5 and §12 vs §15.

The ExpansionistUndersold

V-PaperFlow is a weighted cosine blend. The valuable assets are buried.

  1. The LFO benchmark is the real contribution. Freeze it and release it as "OpenAlex-CS-LFO" with a dataset card.
  2. Split the ground truth into re-citations and discoveries. Discoveries are where novelty shows up.
  3. Evaluate pivot detection on real pivots. Authors whose Paper B drifts away from their past work can label the γ gate.
  4. Onboard users through OpenAlex/ORCID. This solves cold start, and study participants get feeds built from their real records.
  5. Use facets as query operators, e.g. "same task, different method". CSFCube already evaluates this.
  6. Make the NLI-verified explanation rate a headline metric.
The OutsiderInconsistent

Read cold, the way a TA would, the doc contradicts itself before you get to any technical judgment.

  1. V-PaperFlow is defined three ways (§12, §15, §37), and Baseline 2 changes between §14 and §28.3.
  2. The core ablation has no sessions. What is V-SKNN's "position" measured over?
  3. The citation +2.0 tier only points at excluded items.
  4. Symbols are overloaded: γ, λ and wm/d/t each mean two or three things.
  5. The §37 "Fixed" list disagrees with the body:
    • profile fields: 7 vs 5;
    • MMR: plain vs facet-aware;
    • vector store: FAISS vs NumPy/Qdrant;
    • n=50 study with no protocol;
    • API use despite a cost of 0;
    • a 16 GB LLM on a 4 GB GPU.
  6. Presentation:
    • the "no recommendations" header is contradicted by the body;
    • code blocks are left unclosed (§§7, 11, 21, 30, 31);
    • draft text such as "NEED TO BE ATLEAST" is still in;
    • the PaperFlow citation has no title or authors.

Fix: add a notation table, one canonical equation and an honest status list, and spell out how an LFO author becomes a user with a session.

The ExecutorNot runnable yet
  1. Build the eval harness first. §28 can't run as written. Order papers by cited-work publication date and call it a proxy, or demote V-SKNN.
  2. Choose authors first, then crawl. Pick about 500 authors who published in 2023–24, pull their references plus 1–2 hops, fill to 100k, and report coverage.
  3. Close leakage in the day-one code. Use counts_by_year and a graph limited to papers up to T, and reconstruct the inverted-index abstracts.
  4. Cut:
    • the cross-encoder from the feed path;
    • most of the tuning (keep 3 signals and 3 weights);
    • the n=50 study, down to 10–15 people;
    • LLM paraphrasing and the API fallback.
  5. Reconcile §12 and §15, and check that PaperFlow exists and is specified well enough to reimplement.

Order: corpus, harness and baselines (weeks 1–3); GLiNER and SciERC (week 4); the ranker; ablations last.

Peer Review Highlights

All five reviews, condensedA=Executor · B=Outsider · C=Contrarian · D=Expansionist · E=First Principles

Consensus: every reviewer ranked C as strongest, citing its unique catches: the confounded ablation, the mislabelled V-SKNN, the circular +33% result, eδ>1, novelty after MMR, and exact-match facets. A was runner-up every time for its concrete build order. Every reviewer flagged D's blind spot: it praises and expands a benchmark that leaks and can't exercise sessions. One reviewer added that B catalogues defects but never prioritizes them.

What all advisors missed, according to the reviewers:

  • SPECTER2 model-level leakage (raised by 2 reviewers). Its citation-trained embeddings may already encode the held-out links, so only BM25 is clean.
  • Author identity (4 reviewers). OpenAlex merges and splits author profiles, and multi-author papers have no clear "user". Use one seed author per paper.
  • Self-citations in Ref(B) inflate the citation signals (3 reviewers).
  • Statistics (5 reviewers): no sample size, confidence intervals or paired significance tests.
  • Trivial baselines (4 reviewers): popular recent papers before T, co-author papers, recent references.
  • Ethics and consent for the user study (4 reviewers).
  • Compute budget and fallback deliverable, plus how work is split across the team (2 reviewers).
