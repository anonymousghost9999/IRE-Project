PaperFeed

Grounded, time-bounded recommendation of scientific papers, evaluated on what researchers actually cited next.

Phanindra Gollapalli · Dhiraj Chitluri

01 · ProblemPaperFeed

Too many papers. Which ones matter to you?

PersonalBuilt from your own work

The profile comes from the papers you wrote and cited: no questionnaire, no click history needed to start.

ExplainedEvery pick has a reason

Each recommendation says why: shared references, a matching task or dataset quoted from the abstract, or closeness to your profile.

HonestJudged on the future

Hide everything after a cutoff year, then check whether the feed finds what the researcher really cited next.

01 · ProblemResearch questions

Three claims, stated before testing

H1 · AccuracyBeat five baselines

PaperFeed outperforms the 5 Mid-Report baselines: Random, Popularity, BM25, Dense centroid, and Hybrid-Persona on NDCG@10 and Recall@10.

H2 · DiversityWider, not worse

MMR diversification raises topic entropy while losing at most 3% NDCG@10 (non-inferiority).

H3 · SessionsFollow a topic shift

The hybrid persona + session model pivots faster to a new topic and returns to the old one better than simpler user models.

Each test: paired bootstrap over researchers (10,000 resamples), Benjamini–Hochberg correction, Cohen's d. Citation-only evaluated as an extended diagnostic.

A time-bounded slice of the scholarly graph

157,117papers, built author-first from OpenAlex
7.6 Mcitation edges, citing year ≤ 2024

03 · SystemScore and explanation

One score, one honest reason

Score(p) = wpers·persona
  + wcite·log(1 + BC + CC)
  + wfacet·facet match
  + wnov·new method
  + wsess·session − penalty

The explanation is chosen from the term that contributes most. A counterfactual test removes that term and checks that the paper drops.

citationShares 10 references with your paper A quantitative framework reveals ecological drivers…
new methodProposes deep transfer learning techniques, which is new to your work, for testing that you work on
personaClose to your overall research profile
Real examples from the faithfulness audit. Facet and new-method spans are copied verbatim from the abstract at their stored character offsets.

03 · SystemFacet extraction

Facets: verbatim spans, not paraphrases

ExtractionGLiNER, five types

Task, method, dataset, metric, topic. A span is kept only if it appears exactly at its character offsets in the abstract.

Corpus auditMostly on target

  • Grounding 100% (by construction)
  • Density 64% (target 70%)
  • Canonical match 78% (target 80%)

SciERC benchmarkHard task

  • Strict micro-F1 25.6%
  • Relaxed micro-F1 33.7%
  • Core 3 labels, relaxed 41.2%
Grounded ≠ correct: grounding proves the words exist, not that the label is right. Manual precision on our corpus was not measured.
08
03 · SystemUser model

Long-term persona, short-term session

PersonaRecency-weighted

Mean embedding of the researcher's papers, recent ones weighted more.

SessionEMA, α = 0.5

Clicks and saves pull the query toward what they are reading now.

SkipsPenalty

Papers similar to skipped ones are pushed down.

Pivot gateOptional

Reacts faster when the session jumps to a new topic (γ = 0.35).

Track 1 has no clicks, so it tests the persona; the session model is tested only in the Track 2 simulation.

06 · UpgradesWhat the results say

Trade-offs: Graph density vs. real-world utility

Trade-off 1Graph vs Cold-Start

Global graph walks (RP3β, Citation) memorize dense co-citations but fail completely (0.000) on cold-start researchers and brand-new papers.

Trade-off 2Pool depth matters

Expanding the candidate pool (Enhanced M=250) lifts NDCG to 0.0290 and HitRate to 19.4%, outperforming Citation-only on user reach.

Trade-off 3Verifiable grounding

Complex black-box rerankers (RankGPT, LambdaMART) added latency without gain; our linear multi-objective scorer preserves 100% extractive explanations.

08 · IntegrityRecorded deviations

What to read with care

  • 80 tuning researchers give wide CIs: real gains of ~20% could not be confirmed.
  • Unequal tuning budgets across comparison systems (320 grid points for us, 0–12 for others).
  • The test split was reused across runs, so Phase 11 is exploratory.
  • 4 leaked tuning researchers may have nudged the chosen weights.
  • RankGPT ran Qwen2.5-1.5B in fp16 (no 4-bit on the run machine), not 3B.
  • Track 2 used minimax-m3.1-flash via NVIDIA, not the planned Gemini model.
  • Facet labels not hand-checked; canonicalization without lemmatization.
  • No human user pilot yet; the demo's rating export is ready.
Full list: design document §18a, "Recorded deviations of the implementation from this design".

10 · TakeawaysLessons and next steps

What we would do next

LessonsWhat we learned

  • An honest negative result (H1) is still a result.
  • Pre-registration kept us from adopting noise.
  • Audit your own data: our leak flattered our own system.

NextWhere the gains are

  • An RP3β-style graph channel for candidate generation.
  • Keep grounded explanations on top of a graph-first pool.
  • More tuning researchers; a real user pilot.
